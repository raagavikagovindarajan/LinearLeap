📐 Matrix Multiplication

Concept Overview

Matrix multiplication combines two matrices to produce a third matrix by taking dot products of rows and columns. For two matrices $A$ (of size $m \times n$) and $B$ (of size $n \times p$), the product $AB$ is defined only when the number of columns of $A$ equals the number of rows of $B$, resulting in an $m \times p$ matrix. Unlike scalar multiplication, matrix multiplication is generally not commutative — that is, $AB \neq BA$ in general.

Key Formula

Each entry of the product matrix $C = AB$ is computed as:

$$C_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} A_{ik} \cdot B_{kj}$$

That is, the $(i,j)$ entry of $C$ is the dot product of the $i$-th row of $A$ with the $j$-th column of $B$.

Dimension rule: If $A$ is $m \times n$ and $B$ is $n \times p$, then $AB$ is $m \times p$.

$$\underbrace{A}_{m \times n} \cdot \underbrace{B}_{n \times p} = \underbrace{C}_{m \times p}$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Multiply the following $2 \times 2$ matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 1: Identify the dimensions. $A$ is $2\times 2$, $B$ is $2\times 2$, so $AB$ is $2\times 2$.

Step 2: Compute each entry of $C = AB$:

$C_{11}$: Row 1 of $A$ · Col 1 of $B$ $= (1)(5) + (2)(1) = 5 + 2 = 7$

$C_{12}$: Row 1 of $A$ · Col 2 of $B$ $= (1)(0) + (2)(2) = 0 + 4 = 4$

$C_{21}$: Row 2 of $A$ · Col 1 of $B$ $= (3)(5) + (4)(1) = 15 + 4 = 19$

$C_{22}$: Row 2 of $A$ · Col 2 of $B$ $= (3)(0) + (4)(2) = 0 + 8 = 8$

Step 3: Assemble the result:

$$AB = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 4 \\ 19 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$$

✅ Answer: $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 4 \\ 19 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$

Problem 2 — Multiply the following $2 \times 2$ matrices (with negative numbers):

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 1: Both matrices are $2\times 2$, so $AB$ is $2\times 2$.

Step 2: Compute each entry:

$C_{11}$: $(2)(1) + (-1)(-3) = 2 + 3 = 5$

$C_{12}$: $(2)(2) + (-1)(5) = 4 - 5 = -1$

$C_{21}$: $(4)(1) + (3)(-3) = 4 - 9 = -5$

$C_{22}$: $(4)(2) + (3)(5) = 8 + 15 = 23$

Step 3: Assemble the result:

$$AB = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -1 \\ -5 & 23 \end{pmatrix}$$

✅ Answer: $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -1 \\ -5 & 23 \end{pmatrix}$

Problem 3 — Multiply the following $3 \times 3$ matrices:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 1: Both are $3\times 3$, so $AB$ is $3\times 3$. There are 9 entries to compute.

Step 2: Compute each entry row by row:

Row 1 of $A$ = $(1, 0, 2)$:

$C_{11}$: $(1)(1)+(0)(0)+(2)(1) = 1+0+2 = 3$

$C_{12}$: $(1)(1)+(0)(1)+(2)(0) = 1+0+0 = 1$

$C_{13}$: $(1)(0)+(0)(1)+(2)(1) = 0+0+2 = 2$

Row 2 of $A$ = $(0, 1, 3)$:

$C_{21}$: $(0)(1)+(1)(0)+(3)(1) = 0+0+3 = 3$

$C_{22}$: $(0)(1)+(1)(1)+(3)(0) = 0+1+0 = 1$

$C_{23}$: $(0)(0)+(1)(1)+(3)(1) = 0+1+3 = 4$

Row 3 of $A$ = $(2, 1, 0)$:

$C_{31}$: $(2)(1)+(1)(0)+(0)(1) = 2+0+0 = 2$

$C_{32}$: $(2)(1)+(1)(1)+(0)(0) = 2+1+0 = 3$

$C_{33}$: $(2)(0)+(1)(1)+(0)(1) = 0+1+0 = 1$

Step 3: Assemble the result:

$$AB = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

✅ Answer: $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

Key Properties

1. Not commutative (in general): $AB \neq BA$. The order of multiplication matters.

2. Associative: $(AB)C = A(BC)$. You can group multiplications in any order.

3. Distributive: $A(B+C) = AB + AC$ and $(A+B)C = AC + BC$.

4. Dimension compatibility: $A_{m\times n} \cdot B_{n\times p}$ is defined only when the inner dimensions match ($n = n$).

5. Identity element: $AI = IA = A$ where $I$ is the identity matrix of appropriate size.
