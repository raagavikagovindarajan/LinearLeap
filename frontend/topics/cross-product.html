✖️ Cross Product

What is the Cross Product?

The cross product (also called vector product) takes two 3D vectors and produces a third vector that is perpendicular to both original vectors.

📌 Key Property

The resulting vector is perpendicular (orthogonal) to both input vectors.

$$\vec{u} \times \vec{v} \perp \vec{u} \text{ and } \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \perp \vec{v}$$

Formula

For 3D vectors \(\vec{u} = (u_1, u_2, u_3)\) and \(\vec{v} = (v_1, v_2, v_3)\):

$$\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = (u_2 v_3 - u_3 v_2, u_3 v_1 - u_1 v_3, u_1 v_2 - u_2 v_1)$$

Determinant Form

The cross product can also be calculated using a determinant:

$$\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\ v_1 & v_2 & v_3 \end{vmatrix}$$

Standard Basis Vectors

Right-Hand Rule

$$\vec{i} \times \vec{j} = \vec{k}$$ $$\vec{j} \times \vec{k} = \vec{i}$$ $$\vec{k} \times \vec{i} = \vec{j}$$

And in reverse:

$$\vec{j} \times \vec{i} = -\vec{k}$$

Example Calculation

Find \(\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\) where \(\vec{u} = (2, 3, 4)\) and \(\vec{v} = (5, 6, 7)\)

$$\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = (3 \cdot 7 - 4 \cdot 6, 4 \cdot 5 - 2 \cdot 7, 2 \cdot 6 - 3 \cdot 5)$$ $$= (21 - 24, 20 - 14, 12 - 15)$$ $$= (-3, 6, -3)$$

Magnitude

The magnitude of the cross product relates to the area of the parallelogram formed by the two vectors:

$$|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}| = |\vec{u}| |\vec{v}| \sin \theta$$

Where \(\theta\) is the angle between the vectors.

Properties

1. Anti-commutative: \(\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = -(\vec{v} \times \vec{u})\)

2. Distributive: \(\vec{u} \times (\vec{v} + \vec{w}) = \vec{u} \times \vec{v} + \vec{u} \times \vec{w}\)

3. Scalar multiplication: \((c\vec{u}) \times \vec{v} = c(\vec{u} \times \vec{v})\)

4. Self cross product is zero: \(\vec{u} \times \vec{u} = \vec{0}\)

5. Parallel vectors: If \(\vec{u} \parallel \vec{v}\), then \(\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \vec{0}\)

Applications

  • Physics: Torque calculation \(\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}\)
  • Angular Momentum: \(\vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}\)
  • Computer Graphics: Finding surface normals for lighting
  • Engineering: Calculating moments and rotational effects
