✨ Matrix Trace

Concept Overview

The trace of a square matrix is the sum of all elements along its main diagonal (from top-left to bottom-right). It is one of the most important scalar invariants of a matrix, meaning it does not change when the matrix undergoes a similarity transformation $A \mapsto P^{-1}AP$. The trace is also equal to the sum of the matrix's eigenvalues, providing a deep connection between the matrix's structure and its spectral properties.

Key Formula

Definition: For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, the trace is:

$$\text{tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ii} = a_{11} + a_{22} + \cdots + a_{nn}$$

Linearity:

$$\text{tr}(A + B) = \text{tr}(A) + \text{tr}(B) \qquad \text{tr}(cA) = c \cdot \text{tr}(A)$$

Cyclic Property:

$$\text{tr}(AB) = \text{tr}(BA)$$

Eigenvalue Connection: If $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_n$ are the eigenvalues of $A$, then:

$$\text{tr}(A) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \cdots + \lambda_n$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 (Easy): Find the trace of the matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 1: Identify the main diagonal elements.

The diagonal entries are $a_{11} = 3$, $a_{22} = 5$, and $a_{33} = 9$.

Step 2: Sum the diagonal entries.

$$\text{tr}(A) = a_{11} + a_{22} + a_{33} = 3 + 5 + 9$$

Step 3: Compute the sum.

$$3 + 5 + 9 = 17$$

✅ Answer: $\text{tr}(A) = 17$

Problem 2 (Medium): Let

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$$

Verify the cyclic property $\text{tr}(AB) = \text{tr}(BA)$.

Step 1: Compute $AB$.

$$AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1\cdot5 + 2\cdot7 & 1\cdot6 + 2\cdot8 \\ 3\cdot5 + 4\cdot7 & 3\cdot6 + 4\cdot8 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5+14 & 6+16 \\ 15+28 & 18+32 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 19 & 22 \\ 43 & 50 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 2: Compute $\text{tr}(AB)$.

$$\text{tr}(AB) = 19 + 50 = 69$$

Step 3: Compute $BA$.

$$BA = \begin{pmatrix} 5\cdot1 + 6\cdot3 & 5\cdot2 + 6\cdot4 \\ 7\cdot1 + 8\cdot3 & 7\cdot2 + 8\cdot4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5+18 & 10+24 \\ 7+24 & 14+32 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 23 & 34 \\ 31 & 46 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 4: Compute $\text{tr}(BA)$.

$$\text{tr}(BA) = 23 + 46 = 69$$

Step 5: Compare both sides.

$$\text{tr}(AB) = 69 = \text{tr}(BA) \quad \checkmark$$

✅ Answer: Both $\text{tr}(AB)$ and $\text{tr}(BA)$ equal $69$, confirming the cyclic property.

Problem 3 (Harder): The eigenvalues of a $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ are $\lambda_1 = 2$, $\lambda_2 = 3$, $\lambda_3 = 5$. What is $\text{tr}(A)$? Then verify by computing the trace directly for the diagonal matrix with those eigenvalues.

Step 1: Use the eigenvalue-trace connection.

Since the trace equals the sum of eigenvalues:

$$\text{tr}(A) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10$$

Step 2: Construct a concrete example. Consider the diagonal matrix with the eigenvalues on its diagonal:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 3: Read off the diagonal entries: $a_{11} = 2$, $a_{22} = 3$, $a_{33} = 5$.

Step 4: Compute the trace directly.

$$\text{tr}(A) = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10$$

Step 5: Both methods agree.

$$\text{tr}(A) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 10 \quad \checkmark$$

✅ Answer: $\text{tr}(A) = 10$

Key Properties

1. The trace is the sum of the diagonal elements: $\text{tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}$. Off-diagonal elements do not contribute.

2. The trace is linear: $\text{tr}(A + B) = \text{tr}(A) + \text{tr}(B)$ and $\text{tr}(cA) = c \cdot \text{tr}(A)$.

3. The trace satisfies the cyclic property: $\text{tr}(AB) = \text{tr}(BA)$. Note that $AB \neq BA$ in general, but their traces are always equal.

4. The trace is a similarity invariant: if $B = P^{-1}AP$, then $\text{tr}(B) = \text{tr}(A)$. This means the trace is the same regardless of which basis is used to represent the linear transformation.

5. The trace equals the sum of the eigenvalues (counting algebraic multiplicity): $\text{tr}(A) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \cdots + \lambda_n$.
