🔢 Matrix Determinant

Concept Overview

The determinant is a scalar value computed from a square matrix that encodes important geometric and algebraic information. Geometrically, the absolute value of the determinant represents the scaling factor of the linear transformation — for a $2 \times 2$ matrix it is the area of the parallelogram formed by its column vectors, and for $3 \times 3$ it is the volume of the parallelepiped.

The determinant also serves as an invertibility test: a square matrix $A$ is invertible (non-singular) if and only if $\det(A) \neq 0$. If $\det(A) = 0$, the matrix is called singular — its columns are linearly dependent and it maps some non-zero vector to the zero vector.

Key Formula

2×2 Determinant:

$$\det\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = ad - bc$$

3×3 Cofactor Expansion along the first row:

$$\det\begin{pmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & i \end{pmatrix} = a(ei - fh) - b(di - fg) + c(dh - eg)$$

Sign pattern for cofactors:

$$\begin{pmatrix} + & - & + \\ - & + & - \\ + & - & + \end{pmatrix}$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 (Easy): Compute $\det\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$.

Step 1: Identify $a = 3$, $b = 1$, $c = 2$, $d = 4$.

Step 2: Apply the formula $\det(A) = ad - bc$:

$$\det(A) = (3)(4) - (1)(2) = 12 - 2 = 10$$

✅ Answer: $\det(A) = 10$. Since $10 \neq 0$, the matrix is invertible.

Problem 2 (Medium): Compute $\det\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 & -2 \\ 1 & 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ using cofactor expansion along the first row.

Step 1: Label the entries: $a=2$, $b=-1$, $c=0$ in the first row.

Step 2: Compute each $2\times2$ minor by deleting the corresponding row and column.

Minor for $a=2$ (delete row 1, col 1):

$$M_{11} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = (1)(3) - (-2)(4) = 3 + 8 = 11$$

Minor for $b=-1$ (delete row 1, col 2):

$$M_{12} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = (3)(3) - (-2)(1) = 9 + 2 = 11$$

Minor for $c=0$ (delete row 1, col 3):

$$M_{13} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = (3)(4) - (1)(1) = 12 - 1 = 11$$

Step 3: Apply the sign pattern $+, -, +$ and sum:

$$\det(A) = (+1)(2)(11) + (-1)(-1)(11) + (+1)(0)(11)$$ $$= 22 + 11 + 0 = 33$$

✅ Answer: $\det(A) = 33$.

Problem 3 (Harder): Compute $\det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{pmatrix}$ and show the matrix is singular.

Step 1: Expand along the first row with $a=1$, $b=2$, $c=3$.

Minor $M_{11}$ (delete row 1, col 1):

$$M_{11} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 8 & 9 \end{pmatrix} = (5)(9) - (6)(8) = 45 - 48 = -3$$

Minor $M_{12}$ (delete row 1, col 2):

$$M_{12} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 6 \\ 7 & 9 \end{pmatrix} = (4)(9) - (6)(7) = 36 - 42 = -6$$

Minor $M_{13}$ (delete row 1, col 3):

$$M_{13} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 5 \\ 7 & 8 \end{pmatrix} = (4)(8) - (5)(7) = 32 - 35 = -3$$

Step 2: Apply the sign pattern $+, -, +$ and sum:

$$\det(A) = (+1)(1)(-3) + (-1)(2)(-6) + (+1)(3)(-3)$$ $$= -3 + 12 - 9 = 0$$

Step 3: Observe why: Row 3 = Row 1 + Row 2 shifted by 3 each time. In fact, row 3 − row 2 = row 2 − row 1 = $(3,3,3)$, so the rows are in arithmetic progression and are linearly dependent.

✅ Answer: $\det(A) = 0$. The matrix is singular (not invertible) because its rows are linearly dependent.

Key Properties

1. Invertibility test: $\det(A) \neq 0$ if and only if $A$ is invertible.

2. Multiplicativity: $\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)$ for square matrices of the same size.

3. Transpose: $\det(A^T) = \det(A)$, so rows and columns play symmetric roles.

4. Row operations: Swapping two rows negates the determinant; multiplying a row by $k$ scales the determinant by $k$; adding a multiple of one row to another leaves the determinant unchanged.

5. Triangular matrices: The determinant of a triangular matrix (upper or lower) is the product of its diagonal entries.
