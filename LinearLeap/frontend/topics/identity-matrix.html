🆔 Identity Matrix

What is an Identity Matrix?

The identity matrix is a special square matrix that acts as the "1" of matrix multiplication. When you multiply any matrix by the identity matrix, you get the original matrix back unchanged.

📌 Key Property

$$AI = IA = A$$

Where \(A\) is any matrix and \(I\) is the identity matrix of compatible size.

Structure

An identity matrix has 1s on the main diagonal (top-left to bottom-right) and 0s everywhere else:

2×2 Identity Matrix

$$I_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

3×3 Identity Matrix

$$I_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

General n×n Identity Matrix

$$(I)_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i = j \\ 0 & \text{if } i \neq j \end{cases}$$

Example Verification

Let's verify that multiplying a matrix by the identity gives us the original matrix:

Given Matrix

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$$

Multiply by Identity

$$AI = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Result

$$AI = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 0 & 2 \cdot 0 + 3 \cdot 1 \\ 4 \cdot 1 + 5 \cdot 0 & 4 \cdot 0 + 5 \cdot 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = A$$

Applications

1. Matrix Equations: The identity matrix is used in solving systems of linear equations

2. Matrix Inverses: \(A A^{-1} = I\) defines the inverse of matrix \(A\)

3. Transformations: Represents "doing nothing" in linear transformations

4. Eigenvalues: Used in the characteristic equation \(\det(A - \lambda I) = 0\)
