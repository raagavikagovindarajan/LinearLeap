⚫ Dot Product

What is the Dot Product?

The dot product (also called scalar product) takes two vectors and produces a single number (scalar). It measures how much two vectors point in the same direction.

Formula

For vectors in n-dimensional space:

$$\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + \cdots + u_n v_n = \sum_{i=1}^n u_i v_i$$

2D Example

Given: \(\vec{u} = (3, 4)\) and \(\vec{v} = (2, 5)\)

$$\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 3 \cdot 2 + 4 \cdot 5 = 6 + 20 = 26$$

3D Example

Given: \(\vec{a} = (1, 2, 3)\) and \(\vec{b} = (4, 5, 6)\)

$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1 \cdot 4 + 2 \cdot 5 + 3 \cdot 6 = 4 + 10 + 18 = 32$$

Geometric Interpretation

The dot product is also related to the angle between vectors:

$$\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = |\vec{u}| |\vec{v}| \cos \theta$$

Where \(\theta\) is the angle between the two vectors.

Special Cases

Perpendicular vectors: \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 0\) (angle is 90°)

Parallel vectors: \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = |\vec{u}| |\vec{v}|\) (angle is 0°)

Opposite vectors: \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = -|\vec{u}| |\vec{v}|\) (angle is 180°)

Properties

1. Commutative: \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \vec{v} \cdot \vec{u}\)

2. Distributive: \(\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} + \vec{w}) = \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} + \vec{u} \cdot \vec{w}\)

3. Scalar multiplication: \((c\vec{u}) \cdot \vec{v} = c(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v})\)

4. Self dot product: \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{u} = |\vec{u}|^2\)

Applications

  • Work in Physics: \(W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}\)
  • Projection: Finding component of one vector along another
  • Machine Learning: Similarity between feature vectors
  • Computer Graphics: Lighting calculations and surface normals
